The path

Four steps, in this order

Training and paperwork run alongside each other. Neither one waits on the other, and only the final exam is a hard gate.

1

Profile and attestation

Who you are, which dealer number you work under, and your agreement to the standards we hold. Two minutes.

2

Five training modules

Members First, Canvas → Convert → Cultivate, compliance guardrails, scope limits, and field safety. Each ends with a knowledge check at 80%. Unlimited retakes, any order, any sitting.

3

Paperwork

Request your new dealer packet from inside the portal and print the checklist. Work through it while the training continues. Nothing here blocks anything there.

4

Certification exam

Twenty questions, 80% to pass, two attempts. Pass it and your record goes to National Field Operations, then your Territory Manager clears you for ride-alongs.

Roughly 45 to 60 minutes of training, and you do not have to do it in one go. Your progress saves as you work and follows you to whatever device you sign in from next.
What you will need

Have these ready

  • Your dealer number. If you do not have one yet, your Territory Manager issues it.
  • Your sign-in. Issued to you personally by National Field Operations. Never shared between people.
  • A phone, tablet or laptop. The portal works on all three. A phone is enough.
  • Your packet items. W-9, the executed dealer agreement, certificate of insurance, and licence or registration verification for every state you work in. The portal prints the full checklist for you.
Nothing sensitive goes through the portal. Taxpayer identification numbers, banking details and signed agreements are returned through the secure channel named in your packet — never by text, never by email.
The standard

What we train, and why it is not negotiable

Every module exists because getting it wrong costs a member, a licence, or somebody's safety.

Wellbeing before business

Every member contact opens the same way: are you safe, are you in a secure location, is everything okay. Never with billing, never with an account, never with a price.

Member information stays out

No names, addresses, account numbers or panel codes in any field tool or field photo. Identity lives in the system of record, nowhere else.

Four questions you never answer

Code requirements, licensing, contract language, and anything reading as a permit or ordinance issue. Each one routes to the team that owns it.

Canvas → Convert → Cultivate

One doctrine, one wording. Permission first, needs before product, and the job is not finished at sign-off.

Scope discipline

Know where your authority stops. Anything past it gets escalated with a named owner and a due date, the same day, before anything is quoted.

Field safety

You get one body and one reputation. Both are protected before the schedule is.

Questions

Before you ask

How long does the whole thing take?
Around 45 to 60 minutes of training, plus however long your paperwork takes to gather. Most people spread it across two or three sittings. There is no time limit and no penalty for stopping.
What if I fail a knowledge check?
Retake it. There is no limit on module checks, and wrong answers show you the correct one and the reason. The final certification exam allows two attempts; after that it becomes a coaching conversation with Field Development, not a strike.
Do I need internet the whole time?
You need it to sign in and to save progress. The field tools you use afterwards are built to keep working with no signal at all, which is the point of them.
Can I do the paperwork and the training at the same time?
Yes, and that is how it is designed. Paperwork is open from the moment your profile is saved. The only thing that waits is the final exam, which opens once all five modules are passed.
I have not got a sign-in yet.
Your Territory Manager or National Field Operations issues it to you personally. Accounts are never shared between people and are never sent by text or email.
Something is not working.
Every message in the portal carries a code. Read the code to whoever is helping you rather than describing what happened — it tells us exactly what went wrong.

Ready to start?
